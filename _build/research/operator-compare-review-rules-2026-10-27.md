# Shared fixes for all 8 operator compare pages (review round, 2026-10-27)

Three independent review passes (repetition, buyer logic, fact-check) found these on EVERY page.
Every finding below was verified by the lead before being passed on. Apply all of them to your page.

## 1. Cut within-page repetition (the biggest problem)
Count each idea on your page and cut to the limit:
- "<X> stays as it is / is not touched / carries on / nothing changes in <X>": MAX 3 (hero trust item,
  feature row 4, final-CTA H2). Cut it from: how-it-works lead, mid-CTA ("<X> is not part of it"),
  cost FAQ ("your plan stays the same"), problem card 3 tail, final trust items.
- "The two products do not connect / no data passes": state it ONCE fully in the connect FAQ, plus at
  most one consequence elsewhere (e.g. a coexistence card about the trade-off). Remove the closing
  "The two products do not connect." from the comparison lead, the "No data passes between the two"
  row-4 bullet, and "no data moves / nothing flows across" from coexistence card 3.
- "Other suppliers stay in <X> / <X> can order from any supplier": the table row + FAQ 4 only.
- "The invoice still reaches <X>, so the cost is counted": MAX 2.
- "You don't need a <X> alternative; we do one job; if ordering is why you searched, this is enough":
  MAX 2 (FAQ 1 and FAQ 2). The coexistence lead must NOT end with "If <ordering> is what you hoped a
  <X> alternative would bring...". State the division of labor instead.
- "Your distributor loads your products at your prices": MAX 3.

## 2. Coexistence section ("Keep Both" type): 3 cards, all specific to <X>
No generic cards. Do NOT use "other suppliers stay in <X>", "start with one distributor", or "your
costs still include that delivery" as card topics (they were on all 8 pages). Use:
- Card A: the <X>-specific trade-off (promote it from the FAQ): what a spoken order skips or misses in <X>.
- Card B: how to use the two together in practice, in <X>'s own workflow (MarketMan's model: "let its
  pars show what is short, then say that list").
- Card C: who this suits / what to watch. Every page: "Place each distributor's order in one place only,
  so nothing gets ordered twice." PO/approval platforms: "If every order to that distributor needs
  approval, keep it in <X>."

## 3. Problem section ("why look for an alternative")
- Card 3 was the same pitch on all 8 ("your distributor may offer an app"). Replace it with a real third
  reason specific to <X> (your page brief names one).
- Card 2 "leaving is a big job and would not change how one order gets placed" was on 6 of 8: use it only
  if your brief says so; otherwise give a different <X>-specific reason.
- Never say <X> only starts after delivery or lacks ordering: all 8 tools place orders. Say what <X> is
  BUILT AROUND, and that how the order reaches your distributor is a separate step you can change alone.
- No unsourced claims about why people search ("Most people...", "More often...", "usually"): use
  "Sometimes", "Often", or describe, don't generalize.
- Vary the lead's structure; do not open "Looking up/Searching for a <X> competitor rarely/seldom means".

## 4. Hero lead
- Do NOT use the shared 3-sentence template ("Say X into the app, check lines, send, numbered. Distributor
  loads prices. <X> keeps doing Y.").
- Sentence 1 carries the page's angle (the H1's idea) and what the reader gets. Do not open on <X>'s name.
- MUST state the condition: it works only if your distributor offers it.
- MUST say what it adds versus <X>'s own ordering (not versus nothing): e.g. the distributor's own guide at
  your account's prices (not a list you keep or rebuild from invoices), sent through the app your
  distributor gave you, with an order number, time, and a confirmation back to your phone; spoken from the
  walk-in. Pick what is TRUE for <X> per your page brief. ≤50 words.

## 5. The 20-30 minute claim is measured against PHONING the order in
Readers already order inside <X>. Keep the stat (its label must say it is versus a phone call to the rep),
but anchor the page on the distributor you still phone, text, or reach through a rep (MarketMan's page
already does this). Never imply 20-30 minutes are saved versus <X>'s own order screen.

## 6. Comparison table and lead
- Vary the lead; do not use "[Only one thing changes]. One column is... <X> already A, B and C, so a <X>
  alternative is seldom what you need. The two products do not connect."
- Add one short clarity sentence in the lead: the ticks for <X>'s features come from <X>; the voice-ordering
  rows apply to distributors that offer our app.
- No row may imply <X> lacks something it has. A cell "Not listed on their site" is allowed only for a
  real voice/ordering difference, never for something any user knows <X> does (e.g. reviewing an order).
- No ✓ may suggest VoiceOrder Solutions counts stock, sets pars, approves orders, or runs <X>'s features.

## 7. Features H2 must not contradict the H1 ("Not A <X> Alternative")
Never "A <X> alternative that adds one step". Keep the keyword with a non-contradicting phrasing, e.g.
"No <X> alternative needed for this step", "Why this is not a <X> alternative" (each page phrase it
differently).

## 8. FAQs (keep exactly 8, the Webflow slot count)
- Merge setup and cost into ONE FAQ: "How do I get set up, and what does it cost?" Setup: through your
  distributor; if they don't offer it or you're not sure, contact us and we'll connect you with the right
  person (KB homepage wording). Most kitchens place a first order the same day. Cost: a question for your
  distributor, since the app comes from them; we publish no price. State <X>'s pricing fact neutrally
  (published or quote-based). NEVER imply the restaurant pays or does not pay.
- Use the freed slot for ONE <X>-specific question (your brief names it).
- Phrase the "what if a line is wrong" question differently from every other page (it was word-for-word
  on 4 pages). Keep the safeguard answer: you review each line, so a rushed glance can still miss a
  mistake; phone and voicemail orders get no order number from us.

## 9. Mid-CTA and final CTA
- Mid-CTA: a demo cannot show the reader's own guide: "a distributor's guide", "a sample order". No
  "<X> is not part of it" disclaimer. Vary the body from the template "A 20-minute demo: [guide], one
  order said and checked, and what the distributor receives."
- Final-CTA H2: tie it to your page's angle; do NOT use "Keep <X>, and speak/say your next order". Vary
  the body ("Talk to your distributor or to us, and see how an order travels from your phone to their desk"
  was on all 8).

## 10. Claims that are NOT verified (from the KB "Still unconfirmed" list) — remove wherever they appear
- A separate account or guide per location/outlet/site ("each outlet's guide", "each site's lines", "the
  products your site buys", "an outlet guide"). Say "your account", "your kitchen", "your guide".
- Several phones or staff per account ("the kitchen's phones", "staff iPhones", "site phones"). Say "your phone".
- "Their own app" / "your distributor's own app": it is OUR app, which your distributor gives you.
- Same terms / delivery days ("same terms", "same delivery days"): say "same distributor, same rep".
- Prices: "the prices your distributor set for your account" or "your account's prices"; not "negotiated".
- Placeholder testimonials: no numbers or durations at all ("two-minute", "a couple of minutes").

## 11. Copy hygiene
US English; "locations" not "outlets" (except when quoting a competitor plan name); no em-dashes; no
"hands-free"; no "nothing to mishear"; "every order" only for orders sent in the app; no dangling it/they;
8th-grade reading; no clever lines a restaurant owner must read twice; explain jargon (AP, PO, EDI,
punchout) or avoid it.

## Do not change
- Hero CTA label (template-enforced), the stat numbers, the H1 prefix "Not A <X> Alternative:", the meta
  title pattern, the sourcing FAQ's date (27 October 2026), the keyword placements (re-run kwaudit).
