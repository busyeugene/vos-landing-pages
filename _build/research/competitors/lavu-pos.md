## 2. Inventory and supplier ordering (the overlap)
**Lavu has real inventory**, so every one of these is a tick in BOTH columns:
- Vendors and orders (help center, Inventory: Vendors): **"Creating vendors and orders can make adding stock
  to your inventory a fast and easy process. Once you have vendors created you are able to attach them to
  items, and then place orders through vendors for those particular items."**
- **"When creating vendors, the only information that is required is the name and the email address of the
  vendor."**
- ⚠ THE KEY FACT: **"Keep in mind that Lavu Inventory does not have the ability to contact your vendors.
  Lavu will not send emails or contact your vendors in any way."** (support.lavu.com/en/knowledge/inventory-vendors)
- **"you can start using Orders to track shipments that you have placed with your suppliers. Once those
  shipments have arrived at your restaurant, you can receive the order in Inventory to automatically update
  the stock of all of your ingredients that you received."** Orders: "Place new order" -> pick a vendor,
  items, quantities -> **Save** -> **Pending**; receive -> **Closed** or **Partial**.
- Low stock (Inventory Alerts): **"Low stock alerts are triggered once the current stock level, in terms of
  Purchase Units"** falls to the threshold; shown on the Control Panel inventory Dashboard. **86 count
  alerts** use Sales Units and grey the item out on the POS.
- Dashboard: **"Low Stock Level - A running count of items that are at or below their "Low Stock Alert"
  value"**; **"The Purchase Orders section summarizes all pending orders by the Expected Receivables and
  Total Cost of items"**; Pending Transfers **"Applies to multi-location chains only."**; waste value.
- Blog (reordering): **"Lavu's inventory module allows you to set these rules for each item."** and, after
  "Specify preferred suppliers for each product", **"The system can then automatically generate purchase
  orders for the correct vendor."** (generic "the system"; the help center does not describe it). Not used.
- Blog (multi-unit purchasing): **"Lavu integrates sales, inventory, and purchasing data."**; **"Marty
  identifies opportunities to optimize orders."**
- Blog (AI vendor management): Marty **"learns your purchasing patterns and supplier agreements"**, **"flags
  price discrepancies"**, **"suggests alternative vendors with lower prices"**. The page does NOT say Marty
  places, creates or sends a supplier order.
- Integrations page, "Inventory POS Integrations": four inventory partners are listed (one of them has its
  own VoiceOrder Solutions compare page and is banned by claims.js, so name none). Accounting partners
  include QuickBooks. No food distributor or supplier-ordering partner is listed.

⚠ So: Lavu holds vendors, logs orders, receives them into stock, and alerts on low stock. What Lavu says it
does NOT do is reach the supplier: the order still leaves by your call, text, email or rep.

## Angle for the VOS page
**Lavu tracks the supply order; it does not send it.** Its own help center says Lavu "will not send emails
or contact your vendors in any way": you log the order in Lavu to receive it later, and you still have to
get it to the distributor yourself. VoiceOrder Solutions is that sending step, for a distributor that
offers our app: say the order, check each line, send it with a number and a confirmation back. H1: "Not A
Lavu POS Alternative: Send Supply Orders". Distinct from MarketMan ("skip the supplier call": MarketMan
sends orders except to phone-only suppliers) because here the rival's own docs say it never sends.
Second thread: "voice ordering" on a POS means diners calling in; ours is the kitchen ordering FROM its
distributor.

Practical tie-in (sourced): since a Lavu order record contacts nobody, a spoken order can be copied into
Lavu Orders to receive against without ordering twice, as long as it is not ALSO phoned or emailed.
