## Order Agent, the module that overlaps VOS
- Subhead: "Cut order entry time with AI-powered voice, text, email, and PDF order automation."
- "takes orders on any channel and in any format - email, text, photo, and PDF"
- "automatically transforms voicemails, texts, and emails into clean digital orders in Pepper"
- Accuracy basis: "access to order guide and order history powers industry-leading accuracy"
- **No operator-facing ordering app is described for Order Agent.** It parses what the
  customer already sent. No human review step is described on the page.
- **This is the load-bearing distinction.** Pepper works the distributor's inbound side;
  VOS gives the customer an app they open on purpose and confirm line by line before sending.

## Implementation (the strongest honest contrast)
From `/post/how-long-does-it-take-to-implement-pepper`, verbatim:
- **"typical implementation timelines with Pepper range from 35 to 90 days."**
- "Live in 35 Days. Fully Adopted in Weeks." (Pride of the Morning, their fastest published case)
- Five phases: Kickoff & Planning -> Data Setup & Configuration -> App Development &
  Initial Testing -> Soft Launch -> Full Launch & Handoff
- Staffed with "a dedicated Implementation Manager, Forward Deployed Engineer and
  Customer Success Manager"
- Their own framing: "implementing eCommerce technology involves work. It requires
  coordination, familiarity with your ERP..."
- VOS: most distributors up and running in 24 to 48 hours, no IT project. **Cite Pepper's
  own published range, never a guess.**

## They run their own comparison pages
`/compare/pepper-vs-cut-dry`, `/compare/pepper-vs-bluecart`, `/compare/pepper-vs-grubmarket`,
`/compare/pepper-vs-ziizii` — three of those four competitors are also in our batch of 12.

Tactics observed on `/compare/pepper-vs-bluecart`:
- H1 is bare "Pepper vs. BlueCart". Not an HTML table: stacked blocks, one H3 per dimension
  (Ease of Use, Integration, Personalization, Promotion, AI Tools, Sales Rep Tools, Product
  Content, Customer Support), with a "Pepper" entry and a "BlueCart" entry under each.
- Pepper's entries run long and promotional; the competitor's run short and limiting
  ("can feel more traditional", "fewer direct ERP integrations", "does not offer AI tools").
- It concedes the odd strength ("can work for smaller businesses"), which keeps it defensible.
- **It cites no source and no date for a single competitor claim.** Our pages cite and date
  theirs, which is both honest and a differentiator if anyone compares the two pages.
