## Pricing — PARITY, do not attack
Verbatim: **"We charge a monthly fee depending on your usage of Choco. Talk to our sales team to
find a fitting deal for you."** No tier, no number. Also: "We want to make Choco available for
distributors of all sizes, assortments and locations." CTA is "Get started now" into a demo form
promising a reply within 24 hours. VOS is quote-based too, so the pricing-parity rule applies.

## No published implementation timeline
Unlike Pepper (35 to 90 days) and Burnt (1 to 2 weeks), **Choco publishes no go-live time anywhere
we could find.** So rollout length must NOT be this page's lead argument, and we must not guess at
a number. If we mention it at all, it is only that they do not publish one.

## The angle for this page (must differ from the Pepper page)
Choco sells itself on **growth**: an AI storefront with recommendations to "grow every basket",
a sales hub, a foodservice CRM, and a published 20% larger-basket claim. The honest contrast is
**what you are buying it for**, not rollout length:
- A distributor who wants an upsell engine and a CRM is shopping for Choco's shape.
- A distributor who wants the phone to stop ringing and orders to arrive clean is shopping for ours.
- **Scale and fit:** Choco says 30,000 distributors are on it. VOS is built for small to mid-size
  independents and DSD, which is a different buyer, and the homepage says so
  ("Enterprise pricing and enterprise complexity aren't your problem").
- OrderAgent reads **a handwritten note**, which is genuinely impressive and worth conceding. The
  distinction is that nobody in the kitchen confirmed what was read off that note; with VOS the
  person who built the order approves each line before it is sent.
