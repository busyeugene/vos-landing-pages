# OrderEase (orderease.com) — competitor research for `/orderease-alternative`

**Verified live 2026-10-10** (homepage + /pricing). No prior entry in the Writing repo's
competitors file; OrderEase comes only from the October Airtable plan.

## Positioning (verbatim)
- Title tag: **"B2B Order Management Software, ERP Connected | OrderEase"**
- Eyebrow: **"B2B Order Management Platform"**
- H1: **"Take orders everywhere. Rekey nothing."**
- Lead: **"Capture orders from EDI, email, PDFs and portals, validate them against your rules,
  and sync clean orders into your ERP."**
- **"Trusted by suppliers, distributors, manufacturers, wholesalers, and dropship brands"**

## Their own thesis, which this page has to answer head on
They frame the problem as channel sprawl and the answer as one flow:
- Problem: **"One Business, Multiple Order Paths"** — "Each order channel can introduce its own
  rules, exceptions and review points before an order reaches your ERP." / **"Multiple channels
  aren't the problem. Separate workflows are."**
- Answer: **"More Channels Don't Have to Mean More Workflows"** — "OrderEase brings every order
  source into one flow, checked against your rules before it reaches your ERP."
- And explicitly against narrow tools: **"OrderEase covers the whole order path, not one piece of
  it"**, over a grid where an EDI network and a portal each get one column of three and OrderEase
  gets all three.

⚠ **VOS is one piece of it, deliberately.** Do not dodge this. The page should meet it: a
distributor whose problem is the phone does not need the whole path rebuilt. Answer the argument,
do not pretend they did not make it.

## Products and channels
Order Orchestration Hub, Customer Ordering Portal ("B2B Ordering Portal: customer ordering built
for wholesale complexity"), **EDI Automation**, **Email Orders** ("Automate CVS and PDF email
orders" [sic]), **Sales Rep App** ("offline-ready field sales app"), Integration Hub.
Capabilities: ERP Integration ("bi-directional sync"), B2B Ecommerce, Pricing & Promotions
(tiered pricing, customer lists), multichannel Order Capture.

Named featured integrations on the homepage: **NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365, Shopify**.
Integration families: ERP and accounting, shipping and freight, warehouse and inventory,
marketplaces and ecommerce.

The six channel panels, in their words: Wholesale Orders (digital catalogs, customer-specific
ordering rules), EDI Documents ("preconfigured" connections, they handle retailer requirements
and changes), **Emailed POs ("PDF and CSV attachments are read, parsed and routed through the
same workflows")**, Ecommerce (marketplace connectors), Fulfillment Sync (split across
warehouses, fulfillment logic by SKU/channel/location), ERP Integrated ("keeps your ERP as the
source of truth").

## ⚠ Two places where OrderEase is simply better than VOS. Concede both, early.
1. **Emailed PDF and CSV purchase orders are parsed into structured orders.** VOS does not do
   this at all, and our own claim rules say so: a no-retyping claim covers EDI, API and
   QuickBooks, and "emailed PDF, Excel or Word files may still be keyed in by the distributor".
   This is their hero promise ("Rekey nothing") and it belongs in the first table row.
2. **EDI as a managed service.** "We add new trading partners when you need them, without
   restarting the project"; "OrderEase handles retailer requirements and ongoing changes, so your
   team stays compliant without babysitting EDI." VOS sends an order over EDI; it does not manage
   trading-partner compliance.

Also theirs and not ours: marketplace connectors, multi-warehouse fulfillment logic, shipping
software sync, bi-directional ERP sync, a rep app.

## Pricing — ⚠ the "pricing is parity" rule does NOT hold here
OrderEase **publishes a floor**: **"Starting at $500/month"**, "plus a setup fee", and basic
onboarding included, with EDI/ERP/complex workflows quoted as a fixed-price implementation
package. They also state **"No. There's no free version or trial"**.

VOS publishes nothing. So on pricing transparency **OrderEase wins**, and the page must concede
it rather than claim parity.
⚠ **Do not put their dollar figure in the copy.** A published price moves, and a stale number is
the one thing a fact-checking buyer will catch. Say they publish a starting price; let the
sourcing FAQ carry the date. And never pair it with any figure for VOS: stating or implying a VOS
price is banned outright.

## Unaudited numbers — do not repeat as results
"70% fewer manual order touches", "98% reduction in order entry errors", "40% fewer fulfillment
exceptions", footnoted "Based on results achieved by OrderEase customers. Results vary by
implementation." Their ROI calculator also renders a worked "453.85%" ROI. All off limits.
Customer-named case figures (Scotts Canada: 4 manual order channels, 21 sub-brands) are theirs
to claim, not ours to quote.

## Industry fit — accurate wording matters
Their "Built for How Your Industry Actually Orders" list: Lumber/Building Materials and Hardware
(the only one with its own linked page), Apparel & Fashion, **Food & Beverages**, Consumer Goods,
Furniture & Home Decor, Pets, Healthcare, Lawn/Garden & Horticulture.

So food is **one of eight named industries**, not an omission. Named customers: Fence Armor,
Scotts Canada, Century Drill & Tool, Muskoka Brewery, TDI Brands, HJS Wholesale. The homepage's
own dashboard mock-up sells planters, lawn and garden items and drills.

⚠ Write this as breadth, never as "not built for food". They list food and they have a brewery.
The honest form is: horizontal across eight industries, with nothing restaurant-specific on show.

## The angle for this page (must differ from all eight before it)
OrderEase makes every order path cleaner. It is plumbing, and good plumbing: many channels in,
one validated flow out to the ERP. But every channel it consolidates is one where the customer
has already typed something or produced a document, and the work exists because that artifact has
to be interpreted, checked and routed.

VOS goes one step earlier and reduces the number of paths instead of orchestrating them.

> OrderEase makes every order path cleaner. VOS removes the one that makes the most work.

The checkable distinction: on VOS the order is structured at the moment it is spoken, by the
person who wants the goods, who approves the lines before it sends. There is no document to parse
and no inbox step, because nothing was ever a document.

**Do not claim they lack voice ordering as a weakness in isolation** — state it factually. Their
channels are portal, EDI, email, marketplace and rep app, and all of them are typed or uploaded.
**Do not claim VOS replaces them.** A distributor with heavy EDI retailer compliance and a wall of
emailed POs should buy OrderEase, and the page can say so.
