# inFlow Inventory (inflowinventory.com) — competitor research for `/alternatives/inflow-inventory-alternative`

**Verified live 2026-11-09.** Every inflowinventory.com page below was read through the r.jina.ai
reader (same page, rendered to text); none returned an error except the URLs noted as 404. No prior
entry exists. Vendor: Archon Systems Inc., Toronto ("Proudly Canadian"; support "from Toronto and Lisbon").

**Name.** The product is **inFlow Inventory** (siblings: inFlow Manufacturing, inFlow Stockroom). The
brand is "inFlow" (lowercase i, capital F). The registry `competitor` is `inFlow`, so names.js matches
"inFlow", "inFlow Inventory", "inFlow Showroom" and "inFlow Pay" alike; every keyword phrase still uses
the full "inFlow Inventory".

  lack an inventory system, struggle with spreadsheets, or feel stuck on an expensive ERP, inFlow can help".
  **"Great for small and mid-size. From solo entrepreneurs to teams of 50"**. "50K+ businesses" is their
  own unaudited figure: do not repeat it.
- inFlow Inventory page (https://www.inflowinventory.com/inflow-inventory): "For wholesale, ecommerce, and
  field service—or anyone who's having trouble tracking stock." FAQ: "Choose inFlow Inventory if you're in
  field service, B2B wholesale, or distribution."
- Use cases (https://www.inflowinventory.com/use-cases): wholesale, asset tracking, field service,
  warehouse, HVAC, solar/PV installers. **No food-distribution page**: `/use-cases/food-and-beverage-inventory-software`
  is a 404. "Food & beverage" appears only as a tag on **inFlow Manufacturing** on the pricing page.
- inFlow's own round-up (https://www.inflowinventory.com/inflow-vs/the-competition) names a different
  product as "the best inventory software for restaurants and food service". (That product is on the
  claims.js ban list: never name it.) inFlow also positions itself as **"Inventory software vs. a
  company-wide ERP"** (https://www.inflowinventory.com/inflow-vs/erp): it is not an ERP.

## What it does for a distributor (concede all of it)
- Stock: "track inventory from location to location. Scan to check stock, send and confirm transfers, and
  complete full inventory counts"; "which aisle or bin to find it in"; **"Reorder points and
  notifications"**; "Restock how you want" (inflow-inventory page). Wholesale page: "custom units of
  measure let you buy by the pallet, track by unit, and sell by the box"; "set reorder points for each
  product per location ... inFlow can send you reminders and set up the purchase orders for you"
  (https://www.inflowinventory.com/use-cases/wholesale-software).
- Food-relevant: "If you manage food or pharmaceuticals, you can track assigned lots, serial numbers, or
  expiry dates"; "full end-to-end traceability in case of any recalls" (inflow-inventory page);
  "inFlow prioritizes the oldest stock to prevent unnecessary shrinkage" (sales page). Lot numbers are a
  paid add-on on the smaller plans (pricing page): never use that as an argument.
- Sales (https://www.inflowinventory.com/features/sales-and-invoicing): **"inFlow covers everything from
  quote to fulfillment"**; "Create sales quotes or orders from the web or mobile apps, with real-time
  stock"; "Pick, pack, and ship"; credit limits; volume discounts; "know exactly which orders you can
  fulfill now". Accounting: two-way sync with QuickBooks Online and another accounting package (the second
  is on the claims.js ban list; write "accounting sync").
- Barcoding on every plan, its own rugged scanner phone and label printer (homepage, mobile page).
- Wholesale pricing: "multiple pricing schemes", "bulk changes across your entire product catalog".
⇒ inFlow IS the stock system. VOS is not; every inventory row ticks in both columns.

## ⚠ Customer ordering: THEY HAVE IT (Showroom). Do not claim otherwise.
- B2B portal page (https://www.inflowinventory.com/features/b2b-portal): **"Showcase products and take
  online orders with inFlow Showroom"**; "inFlow Showroom is a B2B portal that cuts down on emails with your
  customers"; **"When customers receive a showroom invite they can browse products and add to a cart, just
  like on an ecommerce site. You get to customize their experience, so they'll only see the products and
  prices you want them to."**; "Create unlimited private showrooms"; "Set custom pricing ... for each
  showroom"; "Update stock levels in real time when items are sold"; payments via inFlow Pay (US/Canada);
  abandoned-cart reminder emails.
- Wholesale page: **"No more phone orders or emailing spreadsheets. With just a few clicks, you can create a
  private showroom (B2B portal) for any customer. They'll see only the products and prices you've chosen
  for them, and any order they submit will come directly into inFlow."**
- inflow-inventory page: "B2B sales usually involve a lot of manual work, like calling clients and emailing
  invoices"; "send customers an online catalog with stock levels, pictures, pricing, and more".
- Round-up page: Showroom "lets wholesale customers log in, see their own pricing, browse live stock, and
  place orders that flow straight into inFlow".
- Learning Center (https://www.inflowinventory.com/support/cloud/showroom-inflows-b2b-portal):
  "a simple and customer-friendly **catalog-style view**"; items "in a grid or in rows"; "a shopping bag
  icon ... where the customer can review their order and check out"; on the Checkout page the customer
  "can enter their contact, shipping, and billing details" (pre-filled if on file); "the customer will see a
  confirmation screen showing them the order number"; both sides get a confirmation email; Showroom shows
  "the **available quantity**"; orders can come in **Unconfirmed** for staff to confirm; "Showroom invites
  are valid for two weeks once the initial email is sent". Showroom is a web page; no customer phone app
  for it was found (do NOT claim it is not mobile-friendly: not checked).
- Showroom is an add-on on the two smaller plans and included on the larger ones (pricing page). Never
  used as an argument.
The honest distinction: Showroom is a **catalog the customer browses, a cart and a checkout** (their own
words: "just like on an ecommerce site"). Customers can see live available stock there, which VOS cannot
claim (KB: unconfirmed). VOS is the order spoken into an app from the account's own guide, each line checked.

## ⚠⚠ AI and the MCP server (native)
- Homepage: **"inFlow's built-in AI Report Assistant gets you fast, insightful answers about your orders and
  inventory. Or hook up Claude through inFlow's MCP to create orders or update pricing by just chatting."**
  (Claude is a third-party AI assistant; do not name it on the page: "an AI assistant".)
- Pricing: "AI Report Assistant: Just chat with inFlow's AI Report Assistant to generate custom reports";
  "inFlow API & MCP: API access (including our MCP server) allows you to connect inFlow to other services
  or build custom integrations" (add-on on smaller plans).
- Integrations (https://www.inflowinventory.com/integrations): "There's also an MCP server to help you
  quickly develop your own custom integrations using coding agents".
- Third-party: a community-built inFlow MCP server on GitHub/Glama (bigl34/inflow-mcp-server, pre-release)
  with `upsert_sales_order`; MCPBundles hosts one; Zapier and Integrately list inFlow automations with
  generic AI steps. A web search (2026-11-09) found **no packaged voice ordering product for inFlow**.
⇒ inFlow's AI is for the distributor's own team (reports; an assistant that creates orders when a staff
member types to it). Never "inFlow has no AI". Concede that its API and MCP server let AI or speech tools
be built or connected to create orders, so VOS is not the only way to get there; the difference is a
ready-made app in the kitchen's hands with a line-by-line check before sending.

## Angle for the VOS page
- **inFlow's own promise:** its wholesale page says Showroom means "No more phone orders". Showroom is a
  catalog a kitchen browses, a cart and a checkout ("just like on an ecommerce site"). It suits accounts
  that like to shop that way. A chef standing at the walk-in who already knows what is short would rather
  say it, which is why some kitchens keep calling, and each call becomes a sales order someone types into
  inFlow. VoiceOrder Solutions gives those kitchens a spoken order from their own guide, checked line by
  line. Keep Showroom for the shoppers.
- **Second strand:** inFlow's AI (reports, and an AI assistant that creates orders "by just chatting" via
  its MCP server) works at the distributor's desk, after the chef has already phoned.
- Distinct from the ERP pages (NetSuite web-store add-on, Syspro written orders, Odoo reorder button) and
  from the other inventory-system pages built in the same wave: the argument is browsing vs saying, and
  inFlow's own "no more phone orders" promise.
