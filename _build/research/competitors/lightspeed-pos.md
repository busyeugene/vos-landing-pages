## 6. Real strengths a fair page must concede (biggest first)
1. **It runs the room and takes the guests' orders**: POS, tableside, QR, online ordering, delivery, KDS,
   payments, reservations.
2. **It knows stock from sales**: real-time deductions as dishes sell, low stock alerts, reorder points/pars,
   recipe costing, variance and stock reports (inventory in some plans, an add-on on others).
3. **It already sends supply orders**: purchase orders by email to any supplier, recurring orders, receiving
   into stock.
4. **Lightspeed AI** answers questions about sales, staff and service.
5. Multi-location management; 24/7 support; published plan pricing.

## Angle for the VOS page
**Lightspeed takes the orders coming in; VoiceOrder Solutions carries one order going out.** On a POS page,
"ordering" means guests, so the page says up front that this is the kitchen's order to its food distributor.
Lightspeed already sends a supply order as an emailed purchase order built from a supplier list you keep, and
its own help center tells you what to do when you still phone a supplier: make the PO anyway, skip the
email, receive it for stock. That phone call is the step we change, and only with a distributor that offers
our app: the order is spoken from the distributor's own guide at your account's prices, checked line by line,
and comes back numbered and confirmed on your phone. Lightspeed keeps the floor, the register and the stock.
Not taken by any other operator page: WISK (count drives the order), Craftable (approvals), Apicbase (plan
there), MarketMan (skip the call: closest; ours is "guests in vs supplies out" and "what Lightspeed's help
center says to do for phoned orders"), MarginEdge (invoice after), Orderly (cost vs asked), xtraCHEF (whose
guide), Restaurant365 (it plans, we place).
