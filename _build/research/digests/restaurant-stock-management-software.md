# Digest: restaurant stock management software (operator page)

## 1. SERP makeup
Google reads "stock management" as "inventory management": every result is titled "Restaurant Inventory Management Software".
8 scraped: 4 vendor pages (restaurant365.com, squareup.com/MarketMan, marketman.com, orcainventory.com), 2 listicles
(forbes.com, mitti.com), 2 guides (netsuite.com, touchbistro.com). 100% restaurants, no ecommerce or warehouse.
The searcher is VOS's user (owner, chef, GM) but wants a back-of-house count-and-cost system: counts, recipe costing,
POS depletion, variance, invoices. Only the purchasing/reorder slice overlaps with VOS. Close audience, partial intent.

## 2. How competitors frame the tool
1. Cut food cost / protect margin (7/8): "close the actual vs. theoretical gap" (restaurant365), "Reduce food costs" (squareup), "3%-15% Savings Off COGS" (orcainventory)
2. Real-time stock view (6/8): "Real-time ingredient tracking" (squareup), "Live Inventory Management" (marketman)
3. Save time, automate the manual work (6/8): "faster, easier inventory counts via mobile app" (restaurant365), "automating repetitive tasks" (squareup)
4. Reduce waste (6/8): "Reduce waste before it eats into your profits" (squareup), "spotting and reducing waste" (restaurant365)
5. Integrations with POS / accounting / vendors (6/8): "Easily Connect Your POS, Accounting Software, And Suppliers" (marketman)
6. Never run out mid-service (5/8): "Never run out mid-service" (squareup), "prevent stockouts, substitutions" (netsuite), "Running out of items mid-service" (touchbistro)
7. Order to distributors from the phone (5/8): "Place orders to your distributors faster" (marketman), "Best for Mobile Reordering" (forbes), "place orders to the distributors from within the app" (forbes)
8. Order guides as the ordering backbone (4/8): "pre-built order guides" (restaurant365), "vendor order guides" (marketman, forbes)

## 5. Where our page is weaker
- FAQ 5 names competitors. Reword as "full restaurant inventory platform" and list the SERP's features VOS lacks (counts, recipe costing, POS sales data, variance, waste).
- Comparison: H2 and lead frame rivals as "warehouse systems sold to restaurants". False for this SERP: the rivals are built for restaurants, around counts and food cost. Reframe to count-and-cost platform vs order-first tool; column "Generic Inventory App" should be "restaurant inventory platform"; its order-guide cell becomes "Varies" (restaurant365 has order guides).
- Distributor Stock card and FAQ 4: "shorts" is distributor jargon; say stockouts, real time.
- Hero lead is process-first; can land the outcome (confirmed order, any hour) in the same words.
- FAQ 1 question is sitewide boilerplate ("replace my current system"); operators ask about their count sheet or inventory app.
- Within-page repeats: "no system replacement" x3, "walk the cooler" x2, "your distributor sets" x2, "your food distributor" x2, "your order guide" x2.

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs
- Voice at the shelf: no ranking tool takes the order by voice; they count by phone, then build a PO.
- Set up for you by your distributor, first order same day; SERP tools quote 2 to 4 weeks with menu mapping.
- Nothing to replace: works next to a count sheet or a full inventory platform; changes only how the order is placed.
- 24/7: guides say count after close; VOS lets the order go in right then, when phone lines are shut.
- Confirmed, numbered orders: SERP talks about chasing credits for shorted items after delivery; VOS stops the wrong order before it is sent.
Lean: keep the honest "ordering side" frame, name the full-platform features plainly in one FAQ, and win on setup, voice, 24/7.
