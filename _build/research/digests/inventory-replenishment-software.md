# Digest: inventory-replenishment-software (pass 2)

## 1. SERP makeup
8 pages scraped: 4 enterprise product pages (slimstock.com, peak.ai, toolsgroup.com, manh.com) and 4 articles
(optiply.com listicle, netsuite.com guide, finaleinventory.com guide, netstock.com blog). Industries: retail chains,
wholesale, manufacturing, ecommerce/Shopify/Amazon FBA; food shows up only as case studies (frozen food, "food and
beverage" in an industry list). The searcher is a planner, buyer or inventory manager who wants software to decide
when, what and how much to reorder. Distance to VOS's buyer: medium to far. The overlap is the small to mid-size
distributor's buyer who is tired of finding out too late that an item is low; the SERP's answer (forecast engine,
auto purchase orders) is not what VOS sells.

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs, and how to lean on it
- Starts from the customer order, the first sign of actual demand, captured 24/7 (after-hours, by voice) instead of a
  forecast from last year's history. Lean: "your accounts' orders show demand as it happens".
- A person makes the buying call with current numbers; no black-box purchase orders. Lean: honest, "your buyer decides".
- Live in 24 to 48 hours with order guides you already have; no sales history to clean, no rules to set. Lean in How It Works.
- Built for small to mid-size food distributors, not store networks and multi-echelon warehouses. Lean in comparison lead.
- Sits beside current systems and delivers orders as email (PDF, Excel, Word), EDI, API or QuickBooks. Lean in Q1.
