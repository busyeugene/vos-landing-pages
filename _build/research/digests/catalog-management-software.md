# Digest: catalog-management-software (pass 2)

## 1. SERP makeup
8 chosen, 6 usable scrapes (Kloudville homepage and Salsify glossary failed). One vendor landing page (surefront.com, retail PIM),
four guides/glossaries (pimcore.com, airtable.com, comosoft.us, dealhub.io) and one listicle (theretailexec.com, 10 PIM tools).
Industries: retail, ecommerce brands selling on marketplaces, manufacturing, CPQ. B2B distribution shows up only as a side
mention ("manufacturers and distributors", "custom pricing tiers", "account holders"). No food, no restaurants. The searcher is far
from VOS's buyer: most want a PIM to publish product content to channels. The thin slice that fits is the B2B seller who needs
each customer to see the right items at the right price, and who is tired of sending out sheets. Direct food-distribution
competitors (Choco, Cut+Dry, Pepper, LaceUp) do not say "catalog management"; they say order catalog, storefront, product data,
customer pricing, order template per customer.

## 2. How competitors frame the tool
1. One central home / single source of truth for product data (6/6). "single source of truth for product data" (comosoft.us)
2. Update once, no manual re-sending, changes flow out (5/6). "update information once and automatically propagate" (comosoft.us)
3. Accuracy: no outdated prices or specs (5/6). "prevents mismatched pricing, outdated specifications" (comosoft.us)
4. Inventory sync, stop selling what is out of stock (5/6). "Real-time stock management and prevention of overselling" (dealhub.io)
5. Multichannel syndication to stores and marketplaces (6/6). "syndicate your entire catalog and product data" (surefront.com)
6. Enrichment: descriptions, images, attributes, taxonomy (6/6). "rich media, including digital assets" (surefront.com)
7. Different selections or prices per customer group (4/6). "pricing based on your customer groups" (surefront.com);
   "custom pricing tiers, and/or account-specific services" (airtable.com)
8. Connects to ERP / OMS rather than replacing it (4/6). "Does it replace ERP? No." theme (theretailexec.com)
9. Spreadsheet / printed-catalog pain (4/6). "updated and reprinted every time they made a change" (dealhub.io)
Direct comps: "Your online order catalog, your way" (choco.com); "Messy product data ... creates pricing errors" (cutanddry.com);
"generates an order template for each customer" (laceupsolutions.com).

## 4. FAQ and objection themes
Recurring: PIM vs catalog management (dealhub, retailexec); does it replace my ERP (retailexec); how long to implement
("weeks, not months", retailexec); integrations to look for; can it price by customer group (surefront); AI in catalog
work (airtable, pimcore, retailexec); large SKU counts; pricing ranges (retailexec). Ours answers: replace system, setup
time, per-customer pricing, AI (Tier C), inventory link, small-distributor fit + quote pricing, keeping guides current.
Gaps: no PIM disambiguation (fold into the Tier C AI FAQ), ERP not named in the "replace" question.

## 5. Where our page is weaker
- Hero lead: "they control the convenience" is vague; lead does not say the outcome (every guide current, right price, right SKU).
- Feature 2 H3 "B2B catalog software for every account" is keyword-first; the SERP's B2B hook is customer-specific pricing.
- Feature 1 bullets 1 and 4 say the same thing (no reprinting / no PDFs); no "discontinued" term.
- Feature 3 H3 says voice but not the catalog payoff (orders built from your list, not from memory).
- Feature 4 body is abstract ("stock visibility is tied to order activity"); no "real time".
- Cards lead "No second system for your team to learn" contradicts the admin platform; integrations card omits ERP.
- Comparison: "No system replacement needed" gives a generic catalog tool a ✗, but PIMs sit next to the ERP too; lead does
  not say what generic tools are built for (publishing product content to online stores).
- Placeholder quote "we fix it once" implies one edit updates every guide; not verified.
- FAQ questions are generic ("How long does setup take?", "replace my current system") and shared with many siblings.

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs
- The catalog is delivered as each account's order guide and orders come back on your SKUs: catalog and ordering in one
  loop. PIM tools stop at publishing. Lean on "the catalog your customers order from".
- Built for small to mid-size food distributors; no weeks-long implementation (24 to 48 hours vs "weeks, not months").
- Catalog follows stock automatically, so accounts order what you have.
- Voice ordering from the guide, 24/7, iOS and Android.
- Sits alongside the ERP / distribution software; nothing to replace.
