## 5. Where our page is weaker
- Hero lead is feature-first ("keeps each account's order history, stamped...") with no outcome; SERP leads with "no more guesswork / stockouts".
- Stats: "not an industry average" is a straw man; distributors don't plan from industry averages.
- Problem lead says "roughly is expensive" but never names the two-sided cost planners fear: stock out vs cash sitting in product.
- Feature H2 is audience-first; the page never says the one thing that makes VOS relevant to planners: a plan is only as good as the order data under it.
- Feature 1 bullet 3 repeats its H3. Feature 3 H3 "never sees" is clever but unclear; "can't count" is plainer.
- Feature 4 explains clean lines but not the payoff (no cleanup before planning, history adds up by item).
- Cards H2 "All the tools your buyer needs" overclaims for a tool with no forecast.
- Comparison lead "national networks" is off (SERP planning tools sell to SMB too); row "Cost of being wrong" is vague.
- FAQ 1 and FAQ 8 use the sitewide generic wording (high shared-trigram counts).

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs
- Planning tools need clean history before they help (Finale wants 12 months, standard SKU names). VOS creates clean, dated, per-account order data at the source, on the distributor's SKUs. Lean on this in the feature H2, feature 4 and FAQ 5.
- 24/7 capture: after-hours orders land in the record instead of missing from it (planners call this bad demand data).
- No forecast to trust blindly: VOS shows what was ordered and what is in stock; the buyer decides. Say it as a plus, not an apology.
- Live in 24 to 48 hours vs weeks for planning suites; works alongside ERP/spreadsheet, nothing to replace.
- Built for small to mid-size food distributors, quote-based pricing.
