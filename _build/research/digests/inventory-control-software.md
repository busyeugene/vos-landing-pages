# Digest: inventory control software (distributor page)

## 1. SERP makeup
8 scraped: 4 product pages (Zoho free inventory, Square POS inventory, inFlow home, Sortly home, plus Odoo app page) and
3 listicles (Mitti "best inventory control software", Forbes "10 best inventory management", Procurement Magazine top 10).
Industries: retail, ecommerce/multichannel, manufacturing, warehouse/3PL, general SMB (construction, medical). Distributors
show up only as a segment ("wholesale and B2B", "SMB distribution inventory", "wholesalers and distributors"). No food
distribution page ranks. The searcher is an SMB owner shopping for a stock-counting tool (barcodes, alerts, counts). Far from
VOS's buyer; the overlap is the small distributor who wants to stop running out and stop costly mistakes.

## 2. How competitors frame the tool
1. Real-time stock (7/8): "Manage inventory in real time" (squareup.com); "real-time inventory tracking" (procurementmag.com).
2. Never run out / low-stock alerts (7/8): "Never run out-of-stock" (odoo.com); "alerted when you're running low" (sortly.com).
3. Simple, made for small business (6/8): "Really easy to use" (inflowinventory.com); "Great for small and mid-size" (inflow).
4. Fewer manual entry errors (5/8): "Reduce manual entry and errors" (squareup.com); "reduce manual errors" (procurementmag.com).
5. One place for stock and orders (5/8): "Centralized view" (zoho.com); "one easy app" (sortly.com).
6. Integrates with accounting / QuickBooks (6/8): "QuickBooks Online Sync financial data" (zoho.com); Mitti, Forbes.
7. Protect margin / cut cost (5/8): "protect margins" (squareup.com); "Expense Monitoring" (mitti.com).
8. History / audit trail (4/8): "View Inventory History" (squareup.com); "Audit trail for all stock moves" (forbes.com).
Direct competitors (distributor side): "Eliminate costly order errors" (choco.com); "creates pricing errors" (cutanddry.com);
live inventory checks with substitution suggestions (getburnt.ai, per competitors.md).

## 4. FAQ and objection themes
- "What is inventory control software?" (Square, Mitti, Forbes): ours has "How does it protect margin", close but not a definition. Reword to "what does it do for a distributor".
- Cost / pricing (Square, Forbes, Mitti tables): answered (quote-based).
- Inventory history (Square): answered (look up an old order).
- Integrations / accounting (all): answered (QuickBooks, EDI, API).
- Setup and onboarding fees (Forbes: onboarding cost, implementation fee): answered (24 to 48 hours, no IT project).
- Can it restock automatically / alert me (Square, Sortly): not a VOS capability; "Will VOS stop every short" handles expectation.
- Warehouse / barcode / lots / counts: Tier C FAQ covers warehouse, retail, manufacturing; missing counts, lots, expiry, costing.
- Replace my system / spreadsheets (inFlow, Forbes): answered (no rip-and-replace).

## 5. Where our page is weaker
- Hero lead: abstract opener ("the place margin quietly leaks"). SERP leads with a concrete promise. Name the three leaks up front.
- Stats: "Real-Time / stock behind the catalog..." reads awkwardly; SERP treats real-time stock as table stakes.
- Feature H3s 1 and 2 are feature-first ("built on your order guides", "Cut the shorts that start at the order"). Say the outcome: customers only order what you carry at your price; stop taking orders for product you don't have.
- Feature 2 body has filler ("earns its keep") and never says "real time".
- Feature 3 never says how the order is captured (voice or tap in the app), which is the reason nothing gets re-keyed.
- Feature 4 misses the "easy" framing the SMB SERP expects; proof exists (same-day first order).
- "24 to 48" appears 3x (hero trust, H2, FAQ): within-page repeat.
- Supporting-card H2 and lead are generic chrome ("Everything your team needs", "No extra apps") shared with 5 pages.
- Tier C FAQ lacks the big off-fit SERP themes: cycle counts, lot and expiry tracking, stock costing.
- Final CTA H2 "Ready to stop..." is shared template phrasing; could state the outcome.

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs
- The category controls stock on the shelf (counts, scans, alerts). VOS controls the order, the moment the customer commits to a quantity. Lean on "control starts at the order" in hero, H2s and comparison.
- The customer confirms the order before it reaches you; no SERP tool puts the buyer in the loop.
- Per-account order guides: customers can only order items you carry, at your prices, on your SKUs.
- The mobile app belongs to your customers (voice or tap, 24/7), not to warehouse staff counting stock.
- Sits alongside ERP / QuickBooks, live in 24 to 48 hours; listicles warn of onboarding fees and full-ERP buy-in.
- Built for small to mid-size food distributors, not retail, ecommerce or factories.
