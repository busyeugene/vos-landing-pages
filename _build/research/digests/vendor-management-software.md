## 1. SERP makeup
All buyer-side tools. Procurement / P2P suite (Precoro), contract lifecycle + vendor module (Cobblestone), unified vendor risk + CLM (Gatekeeper), third-party-risk tools for banks and credit unions (Quantivate, Tandem), plus Gartner reviews, Rippling / Mitti / Vertice / ComplianceQuest listicles and an SAP explainer. Industries: banking, healthcare, government, mid-market procurement; restaurants appear only as "Hospitality" in one industry menu. The searcher is the BUYER (procurement, compliance officer) rating vendors. The food distributor is the vendor inside these tools. Distance to VOS's buyer: very far, which is why the page stays a vendor's-seat reframe. The useful signal is what buyers score vendors on.

## 2. How competitors frame the tool (5 usable pages)
1. One central vendor record / single source of truth: 5/5 ("central repository of vendor data", tandem.app; "One platform. One source of truth", gatekeeperhq.com)
2. Contracts, key dates, renewal reminders: 5/5 ("Never miss a renewal", precoro.com; "automated reminders", tandem.app)
3. Risk, compliance, due diligence, audit-ready: 4/5 ("score your vendors on multiple variables", quantivate.com)
4. Vendor performance ratings / scorecards: 4/5 ("Know which vendors deliver", gatekeeperhq.com; "Vendor ratings for better performance", cobblestone)
5. Replace spreadsheets, calendars, email back-and-forth: 4/5 ("Avoid complicated spreadsheets", tandem.app; "Minimize back-and-forth emails", precoro.com)
6. Make vendors easy to work with (portal, vendors keep catalogs current): 3/5 ("Make it easy for vendors to work with you", precoro.com)
7. Audit trail / who did what, when: 4/5 ("clear audit trail", precoro.com; "automatic audit trail", gatekeeperhq.com)
8. Quick start, easy to use, no IT: 3/5 ("No IT tickets", precoro.com, but "2–8 weeks" to go live)
Buyer criteria stated outright (Precoro FAQ): the right goods, on time, at the right price, up to standard; avoid "late deliveries or poor quality"; consolidate vendor lists to get better terms.

## 5. Where our page is weaker
- Hero lead: three long sentences; promise ("easiest name on that list") is fine but never names what buyers judge (price, accuracy, ease, records).
- Features H2 is defensive ("is for buyers. VOS is for the vendor."), not outcome-first. SERP says buyers rate vendors; we can say VOS works on what they rate.
- Feature eyebrows / h3s describe features, not the buyer criterion each row wins on (right price, clean order, paper trail, reps who answer).
- Feature 3 Tier B sentence is awkward ("settle questions like that"), and bullet "The same record for you and your customer" goes past the verified fact (customers get confirmations; shared history is not confirmed).
- Problem lead does not mention the real risk the SERP shows: buyers trim vendor lists.
- FAQ "Can I see what each account ordered" asks it feature-first; searchers think "record / proof if an order is questioned".
- Staffing / risk FAQ is vague ("in other fields"); SERP shows it is banks and regulated firms doing due diligence.
- Comparison "Setup time: Months" for a procurement suite is overstated; Precoro says 2–8 weeks.

## 6. Where VOS genuinely differs
- It sits on the vendor's side. Every SERP tool is bought by the buyer; VOS is bought by the distributor and handed to the restaurant. Lean on: "their tool rates you; VOS helps you rate well on ease, accuracy, price and records", without a scorecard claim.
- No procurement team needed: a chef or manager orders by voice in an app, 24/7. SERP tools assume finance, legal and procurement staff.
- Live in 24 to 48 hours, customers ordering the same day, vs weeks for SERP tools. No rip-and-replace: orders land as PDF / Excel / Word email, EDI, API or QuickBooks.
- Per-account order guide at your prices and SKUs = the "up-to-date catalog" buyers want from a vendor, kept by the vendor.
- Versus direct rivals (Choco, Pepper, Burnt ingest scattered voicemail / email / text; Cut+Dry is a storefront with payments): VOS is a dedicated app the customer's own staff speak into, built for small to mid-size distributors. Do not name them on the page.
