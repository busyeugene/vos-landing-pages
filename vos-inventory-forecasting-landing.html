Sound Familiar?

A forecast is arithmetic. The hard part is what you feed it.

Distributors rarely struggle with the math. They struggle because the history underneath it was never recorded properly:

🗒️

"It lives on invoices, not orders"

You know what shipped. Nobody kept a record of what was asked for.

📅

"Everything is dated Monday"

Calls from Friday and Sunday all get keyed in together, so the shape of the week disappears before anyone sees it.

🧮

"We bought the tool and it guessed"

Point a clever model at thin, retyped history and it produces confident nonsense. Nobody trusts it twice.

Better Input

What your inventory forecasting software is working from

Demand, Not Invoices

Give an inventory forecasting tool what customers asked for.

An invoice records what left the building. The order records what the kitchen wanted, including the lines you had to substitute or could not fill. Feed inventory forecasting software the second one and you are modeling demand rather than modeling your own shortages.

  • ✓The quantity requested, as the customer said it
  • ✓Each line against the code you sell it under
  • ✓Confirmed by the buyer before it reached you
  • ✓Kept per account, so patterns are per customer

The Clock Matters

Orders dated when they happened, not when they were typed.

Each order from the app arrives with the hour it was sent. Weekend and late-night demand stops collapsing into Monday, which is usually the first thing inventory forecast software gets wrong about a food business.

  • ✓A timestamp from the moment of ordering
  • ✓Its own reference number, for tracing one back
  • ✓Nothing waits in voicemail to be entered
  • ✓Captured whether or not your office is open

Today's Shelf

See what is on hand while orders land.

A projection is one half of the decision. VOS shows live stock that shifts with the ordering, and customer guides move when levels do. Any inventory forecasting solution looks at next month; this shows what is on the shelf now.
