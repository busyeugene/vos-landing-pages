Sound Familiar?

There is a process. It lives in four people's heads.

Ask two staff how an order gets from a chef to the pick list and you get two answers. That is not a documentation problem, it is a path problem:

🔀

"It depends who picks up"

One takes it on a pad, one texts it to the warehouse, one remembers to enter it. Same business, three paths.

🎒

"Only Dave knows that account"

Which items they get, what price, who to call about a short. When Dave is off, the account gets guesswork.

🫳

"It fell between two people"

The order was taken and never entered, or entered twice. Nobody can tell you where it stopped, because nothing recorded the handoff.

Handoffs On Record

You can tell where an order got to.

Anything out of the app has a reference, a stamped time and the account attached, so "did that come in?" has an answer. Any distribution workflow solution worth running has to make the handoff visible, and that is the piece a telephone call never provides.

  • ✓A reference number on anything sent from the app
  • ✓The buyer gets a confirmation when it lands
  • ✓Kept by account on one screen
  • ✓Phone orders stay invisible to this, as they always were

Simple Setup

Agreeing the path takes one conversation

There is nothing to diagram and no process to write up. You tell us two things, we do the rest.

1

What each account can order

A guide per customer: items, your prices, your codes. However it is held today usually works.

2

Where finished orders land

The same destination for every account: an inbox, QuickBooks, EDI or the API. That decision is the whole workflow design.

3

We set each account up and off it goes

For most distributors the whole thing takes 24 to 48 hours. Buyers get the app and usually order the same day.
